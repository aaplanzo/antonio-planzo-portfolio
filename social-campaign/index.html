The brief

Big news, few details. Say both out loud.

On Sept. 30, 2026, at Marine Corps Base Quantico, Secretary of War Pete Hegseth announced the Autonomous Warfare Command, or AUTOWARCOM: a planned four-star command to develop, buy and field drones, robots and other autonomous systems across the joint force. The target date is Oct. 1, 2027, and Congress has to pass a law to create it. Until then, Project Agincourt is laying the groundwork.

Interest is high and details are thin. Nobody has been named to lead it, and its headquarters and budget haven't been announced. That gap fills with speculation, hype and fear unless the department fills it first.

So the campaign has one rule: explain what's decided in plain language, and say plainly what isn't decided yet.

Decided, as announced

  • A four-star, functional combatant command, organized around a mission rather than a region
  • Scope: air, land, sea, undersea and space, plus software, sensors and communications
  • Competitions, exercises and combat performance decide which systems advance
  • New military specialties in each military department
  • Target date of Oct. 1, 2027, pending legislation

Not decided yet

  • Who will lead it
  • Where it will be based
  • Its budget and final authorities
  • Details of the new specialties
  • Technical standards and acquisition priorities

Calls to action

Every post points to one official page with the announcement, the Project Agincourt letter and updates. Industry questions go to the project's official contact, job questions go to each service, and media questions go to department press operations.

Rules for breaking news

Post only after the official release or transcript is live, and link to it. Quote it exactly. Say what isn't decided. Don't speculate about names, places or numbers that haven't been announced, and don't comment on a nomination or a bill beyond the facts.

  • Corrections are targeted. Answer where the claim is spreading, not everywhere.

New formats for the AUTOWARCOM campaign: adopt, pilot or skip

I looked at five formats against three tests: does it serve the campaign's goal, will it still matter next year, and can we run it within our approval, accessibility and records rules?

FormatRecommendationWhy
Vertical short-form explainersAdoptOne edit runs as YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and Facebook video. A durable format, not a trend, and it reuses work we're already doing.
Instagram Stories quiz stickersAdoptBuilt into the platform, cheap to make and easy to measure. Each wrong answer shows which fact needs more work.
Live Q&A with Project Agincourt leadersPilotRun once, on YouTube with a Facebook simulcast. Answers real questions, but needs speaker time, live moderation and cleared policy language ready.
AR filters and 3D models of dronesSkipCostly to build, short shelf life, and it would put a face on systems that haven't been chosen.
AI-generated images or video of autonomous systemsSkipGenerated visuals could be mistaken for real systems in a conversation already full of speculation. Official imagery stays real and credited.

Barriers and how to clear them

The live Q&A needs speakers booked two weeks out and cleared answers on the use-of-force question before going live. Quiz results need a weekly export, since Stories data expires from the app.

Sharing what we learn

After Dec. 4, a short lessons-learned brief goes to military department and combatant command social media managers: which formats built understanding, the reply bank and the decision rules that worked.

Approval flow

  1. By Oct. 30DraftSocial team drafts the plan, posts, graphics and reply bank.
  2. By Nov. 2Fact checkProject Agincourt confirms every fact, term and link.
  3. By Nov. 3Legal reviewGeneral counsel and legislative affairs review posts about Congress.
  4. Nov. 4Approve and shareDepartment public affairs approves; services and commands get the toolkit five days before launch.
  5. Nov. 9Launch and monitorPosts go live; comments are monitored daily, with same-day escalation for anything off-script.

Rules that keep messaging consistent

Same facts and wording everywhere. Explain that Congress must act, but never ask the public to contact Congress; federal anti-lobbying rules bar it. Link to official pages, never to third-party summaries. Anything not in the reply bank goes to department public affairs before we answer.
