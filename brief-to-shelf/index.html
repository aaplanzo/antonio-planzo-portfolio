03 · Partner playbook

House rules for
working with Creative

The planner tells you when. These tell you how: the five gates every project passes through, what a complete brief looks like, and what "ready for vendor" means.

1
Brief sign-off

The brand owner and Creative Ops agree on scope, date, and budget. Nothing starts before this.

2
Concept select

One route is chosen. Changing direction after this resets the clock.

3
Copy lock

Legal, regulatory, and claims copy are final before final art begins.

4
Release to vendor

Files are preflighted, the proof is approved, and the estimate is signed.

5
Quality sign-off

The press check or line trial passes, and the finished goods match the proof.

The recipe: a complete briefBring all 7
  1. Business objective: the one thing this work has to do
  2. Audience and channel: who sees it, and where
  3. Deliverables with sizes, SKUs, quantities, and languages
  4. In-market date and hard gates, such as sales meetings or distributor deadlines
  5. Mandatories: legal copy, claims, logos, UPCs, dielines
  6. Budget range and cost center
  7. Approvers named as people, not departments
Quality: ready for vendorCheck before release
  1. Correct dieline, bleed, and color profile; fonts packaged or outlined
  2. Barcodes scanned and verified against the SKU master
  3. Brand standards checked: lockups, color, clear space, type
  4. One consolidated feedback round per review, with no side-channel edits
  5. Proofs approved in writing; press check held for new packaging
  6. Files, proofs, and estimates archived to the shared library at close-out
